Living together

House rules the whole flat will actually follow

Rules stuck on the fridge don't work. One page that everyone helped write does. Here is what goes on it, a version you can copy, and how to raise the things nobody wants to raise.

The short version

  • Write rules together, in the first week, while everyone still likes each other.
  • Keep it to one page. Seven headings cover almost every fight a flat has.
  • A rule needs a default: who does it, by when, and what happens if it slips.
  • Raise things early and small. "Can we talk about the bins?" beats a note on the fridge.
  • Look at the page again after a month. The first version is always wrong somewhere.

Every flat has house rules. In most flats they are unwritten, different in each person's head, and discovered one argument at a time.

Writing them down sounds formal for four people sharing a 2BHK. It isn't. It is fifteen minutes in the first week that saves you the conversation that starts, "Okay, I didn't want to bring this up, but."

Why rules on the fridge fail

Three reasons, and each has a fix.

  • One person wrote them. So they read as that person's demands. Write them together, and let everyone add one thing they care about.
  • They are too vague. "Keep the kitchen clean" means nothing, because everyone already believes they do. "Dishes washed the same day" can be checked.
  • Nothing happens when they slip. Not a fine. A default: if the bins aren't out by 9, whoever notices says so in the group, and it is done that evening.

The seven things worth writing down

You don't need a rule for everything. These seven cover almost every falling-out.

1. Cleaning

Who cleans what, and how often. If there is a cleaner, what she does and doesn't do, and who covers the days she is away. A rota that turns every week is fairer than fixed jobs, because nobody is the bathroom person forever.

2. The kitchen

Dishes, the sink, the stove. Whose shelf is whose in the fridge. What is shared (oil, salt, washing-up liquid) and what is yours. Any food rules for the shared kitchen and the shared pans.

3. Guests

Friends over in the evening: fine without asking, or a message first? Overnight guests: how much notice, and how many nights in a row before it becomes a conversation? A partner who is there four nights a week is, in practice, another flatmate, and it is fair to say so.

4. Noise and quiet hours

A time after which the hall goes quiet on weeknights. Calls on speaker. The TV. Somebody's early flight, somebody's night shift.

5. Shared things

The washing machine, the AC remote, the Wi-Fi. What you may borrow without asking (the iron) and what you may not (the laptop charger, the last two eggs).

6. Money dates

When the rent leaves each account. Who pays the owner. When the bills are split. The numbers themselves belong in the money agreement: how to split the rent and the bills.

7. How we raise things, and how we leave

The most useful line on the page: "If something is bothering you, say it within a week, in person or in the group, and nobody gets to be offended." And the notice everyone gives the flat before moving out.

A rule is something you can check. "Be considerate" is a hope.

One page you can copy

Change the words in brackets. Delete what doesn't fit your flat. It is not a legal document and isn't meant to be one: it is the flat's memory.

Our flat, on one pageSend on WhatsApp
OUR FLAT, ON ONE PAGE
Agreed on [date] by [names].

CLEANING
Rota turns every [Sunday]: kitchen, bathroom, hall and bins, one each.
The cleaner comes [days]. On her days off, the rota covers it.

KITCHEN
Dishes washed the same day. Nothing left in the sink overnight.
One fridge shelf each. Shared: [oil, salt, sugar, washing-up liquid], bought in turn.
[Any food rules for the shared kitchen.]

GUESTS
Friends over: a message in the group first on weeknights.
Staying the night: say so [a day] before. More than [three] nights in a row, we talk first.

QUIET HOURS
[11 pm to 7 am] on weeknights: headphones, doors closed, no calls in the hall.

SHARED THINGS
Washing machine: not after [10 pm]. AC and geyser off when you leave the room.
Ask before borrowing anything that isn't in the shared list.

MONEY
Rent leaves our accounts by the [3rd]. [Name] pays the owner on the [5th].
Bills are split on the [1st] as agreed.

IF SOMETHING BOTHERS YOU
Say it within a week, in person or in the group. Nobody gets to be offended.
We look at this page again on [date, a month from now].

LEAVING
[One month]'s notice to the flat. You help find the next person.

Bringing up the awkward thing

The page won't stop every problem. It makes the conversation shorter, because you are pointing at something everyone agreed to, not at a person.

Four habits that make it easier:

  1. Early and small. The first time, it is a two-line message. The fifth time, it is a fight with a list.
  2. In person, or in the flat's group. Not in a private chat with the third flatmate, and never on a sticky note.
  3. Describe what happened, then ask for one thing. Not what kind of person they are.
  4. Offer a way out. People change what they do far faster than they admit they were wrong.

Instead of

You never clean. I'm tired of living in a mess.

Try

The bathroom was skipped two weeks running. Can we swap weeks if this one doesn't suit you?

Instead of

Your girlfriend basically lives here now.

Try

Priya's here most nights now, which is fine. Can we talk about the bills, since it's four of us most of the week?

Instead of

Some of us have work in the morning.

Try

I'm up at six this week. Could the TV move to your room after eleven?

A message to send when the rent is lateSend on WhatsApp
Hey, the rent's due to the owner on the 5th and I haven't got your share yet. Can you send it today? If this month is tight, tell me now and we'll work something out before it reaches the owner.
Give it a month

Put a date on the page to look at it again. The first version always gets something wrong, usually the rota. Changing a rule everyone finds silly is how the other rules stay alive.

What not to write down

A flat with forty rules is a hostel with a warden. Leave out anything that happens in someone's own room with the door closed and reaches nobody else. Leave out anything aimed at one person: if a rule exists because of Rohan, talk to Rohan.

And leave room to be decent. The best flats aren't the ones with the best rules. They are the ones where somebody takes the bins out when it isn't their week and doesn't mention it.

Rules work when you start with people who live roughly the way you do. What to ask a flatmate before you say yes is the conversation to have before any of this.
