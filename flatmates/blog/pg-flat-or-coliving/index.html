Finding a place

PG, flat-share or co-living: which one fits you?

There are three ways to rent a room in an Indian city, and they differ less in price than in how much of your own life you get to run. Here is an honest comparison, and five questions that settle it.

The short version

  • A PG is the least work and the most rules. A flat-share is the most freedom and the most work. Co-living sits between, and you pay for the service.
  • Compare the whole monthly cost, not the rent: food, electricity, Wi-Fi, the cleaner, the commute.
  • Staying under six months? Lean PG or co-living. A year or more? A flat-share usually wins.
  • In a flat-share, the flat matters less than who is in it.

You have a job offer or a college seat in a city you don't know, and three kinds of listing in front of you. They all say "furnished room". They are three quite different lives.

"Usually" is doing honest work in that table. A PG run by a relaxed family and a co-living building with a strict warden both exist. Ask; don't assume.

PG: least work, most rules

A paying-guest room is the fastest way to land in a city. You arrive with a suitcase, there is a bed, and dinner appears.

What you trade for that is control. The owner decides who else lives there and, often, when the gate closes, who may visit and what is cooked. Those restrictions are the drawback to weigh.3

On paper, a PG stay is usually a licence to occupy a room, not a tenancy.2 In practice that means it is easy to move in and out, and just as easy to be asked to leave. Read whatever you are asked to sign, and ask the two questions that matter: how much notice either side gives, and when the deposit comes back.

If you are looking at a PG in Bengaluru

The city's civic body set rules for PGs in August 2024: CCTV at the entrances, round-the-clock security, at least 70 square feet a person, fire-safety clearance, and a food-safety licence where meals are served.5 It is fair to ask a PG whether it meets them.

Flat-share: most freedom, most work

You and one to four other people rent a flat. Nobody tells you when to come home or what to cook. Nobody arranges the Wi-Fi, chases the plumber or notices the gas has run out, either.

It is the only one of the three that feels like a home of your own. What it costs you is different in kind:

  • A bigger deposit. Custom varies a lot by city, and a rented flat usually asks far more than a PG or a co-living room.1 There is no national cap; the deposit rules, explained.
  • Real paperwork. A rent agreement, and your name on it.
  • Bills to split , and a system for splitting them. A calculator for the rent and a table for the bills.
  • Each other. A flat-share is only as good as the people in it, and unlike a PG, nobody else is going to manage them for you.

That last one is the whole game. What to ask a flatmate before you say yes, and once you are in, house rules the whole flat will actually follow.

Co-living: in between, for a price

Co-living is a furnished room in a building run by a company, with the dull parts of a flat-share done for you: Wi-Fi, housekeeping, common areas, laundry rooms, security.4 You get more freedom than in most PGs and more service than in any flat.

You pay for the convenience, and you give up the choice of who is on the other side of the wall. Before you book:

  • Get the full monthly figure with every add-on: meals, electricity above a limit, laundry.
  • Read the exit terms. Notice, lock-in, and how many days after you leave the deposit is refunded.
  • Visit the actual room , not the sample one.

A word on GST

Two facts worth knowing, because bills sometimes surprise people.

  • Rent on a home you live in doesn't carry GST. Renting a home to a person for their own use is exempt.6
  • PG, hostel and co-living charges are exempt up to ₹20,000 a person a month , when you stay at least 90 days in a row.7 If "GST" appears on a smaller bill for a long stay, ask what it is on.

Five questions that settle it

  1. How long are you staying? Under six months, a flat's deposit, paperwork and set-up are hard to justify. A year or more, and they pay for themselves.
  2. How much do you want to run? If chasing a plumber after a long day sounds unbearable, be honest about that now.
  3. Do you want to choose who you live with? Only a flat-share lets you.
  4. How do you eat? If you would rather not cook, count the cost of a cook or of eating out before a flat looks cheap.
  5. What does the whole month cost? Not the rent. Everything.

A common path: a PG or co-living room for the first two or three months while you learn the city, then a flat-share with people you have actually met. There is no prize for getting it right on day one.

Whichever you choose, check the place properly before you pay anything, and know what a rental scam looks like.
