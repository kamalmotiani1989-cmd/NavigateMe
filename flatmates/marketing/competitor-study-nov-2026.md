# FindMeFlatMate · Competitor study · flat-rental and flatmate companies and their ads

Prepared by Mark for Kamal's Sunday 9 November 2026 sit-down. Task set in `flatmates/team/priorities.md` (Mark, item 1).
India first, then four abroad. Facts only; every fact carries the URL where it was seen. **All sources were seen on
6 November 2026** unless a different date is written next to them. Nothing was spent, posted or signed up for.

**How this was researched, and the limit to know about.** This session's network allows no direct page reads (every
website, the Play Store, the App Store, Instagram, facebook.com and the Meta Ad Library were refused). Every fact
below comes from search-result excerpts of the cited page or from third-party coverage. So: homepage wording is
"as excerpted", prices are "as shown in the excerpt", and three things could not be checked at all:
**Google sponsored ad copy, the Meta Ad Library, and Instagram bios and captions.** Section 4 says how to close
those gaps in an hour on Kamal's own phone. Where sources conflict, both figures are given and marked.

## 3 · Recommendation (one page)

**What the market looks like, in four lines.** The big Indian portals sell "no brokerage" and now make most of their
money from paid plans that reviewers call misleading, with hidden prices and call floods. PG and co-living brands sell
"zero deposit" and then lose trust on deposit refunds. Nobody large in India scores compatibility; the two that try
(FlatMate.in, FlatX) are small and Bengaluru or Delhi first. Abroad, the leaders monetise by gating contact
(Early Bird, Bold, Plus) and get hammered in reviews for it; the one with our request-and-accept model, Badi, charges
the lister to accept.

**What we copy**
1. **One enemy, said plainly, for years.** NoBroker has run "a month's rent for a phone number" since 2019 and it
   still works. Keep "No brokers, ever" on every piece; never dilute it with five benefits.
2. **The reverse listing as a headline product.** SpareRoom's "Rooms Wanted" and Flatmates.com.au's "room-wanted"
   profiles are the owners' reason to come. Our Escape list is the same thing with a vibe score on it. Lead owner ads
   with "see who's looking near you before you post", as the Pune plan already does.
3. **Seasonal campaigns tied to moving season.** Flatmates.com.au runs one summer campaign a year with one line
   ("Find your people"). Ours is the December and January notice-period season; one line per season, every channel.
4. **Badi's clarity: request, accept, match.** Three words explain our whole model. Use "it's a match" language in
   seeker ads; it is already how Tribe celebrates.
5. **A parent audience for freshers.** Stanza's "Approved by Moms" worked for PGs. For us: "owners choose who they
   meet, numbers swap only on a yes" is the line that reassures a parent, told in a fresher-season post. No safety
   claims beyond the spec.
6. **The referral habit.** Stanza runs a refer-and-earn app; we have "2 friends = 1 free contact". Put it in the
   app-open comms at launch; it costs nothing.

**What we do differently, and the ad each one gives us**
1. **Prices on the page, one-time passes, no subscription.** Housing.com, Magicbricks and 99acres hide prices
   behind login; Roomster, Flatmates.com.au and Badi draw "unauthorized charges" reviews from subscriptions. Ours:
   ₹500 passes, one-time, GST shown. Ad: "₹500. Once. No subscription. Price on the page."
2. **Contacts are used only when an owner says yes.** NoBroker's most common complaint is contacts burnt on
   occupied listings. Ad: "Five free contacts. Used only when an owner says yes. Never on a dead listing."
3. **Never gate the reply.** Early Bird and Badi Plus make people pay to answer; every one of those brands bleeds
   one-star reviews for it. Accepting a request is free for owners, always. Say it: "Owners never pay to say yes."
4. **Both sides answer the same 12 questions.** Filters are not matching. No Indian portal and no PG brand scores
   fit; FlatMate.in shows a percentage without a visible method. Ad: "Not a filter. The same 12 questions, both
   sides, scored."
5. **Your number stays yours.** "16 to 20 calls a day" (NoBroker), "50 calls" (Magicbricks), "10 sales reps in 24
   hours" (99acres) are the words in their reviews. Keep "Your number stays yours" as a standing ad; it is backed by
   their weakness, not our opinion.
6. **The deposit moment.** Every PG brand is fighting deposit-refund complaints; the replacement persona's "deposit on
   the line" is the right fear to meet. Keep P2 at 30% of budget.
7. **Never buy or stage reviews, never fake activity.** Roomster paid $1.6M for it and its reviews still say the
   messages are fake. Real numbers from the report or none.

**Why.** Each copy is a tactic a leader has run for years in public; each difference is a complaint their customers
write in reviews that our spec already answers. The Pune plan needs two additions, both copy only: an ad on
"contacts used only on a yes" and an ad on "one-time pass, no subscription", to test in weeks 2 and 3.
