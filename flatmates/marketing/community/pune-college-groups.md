# Pune college groups · how to get in for zero rupees

_Written by Salu for Kamal's Sunday 9 Nov sit-down. Public web research of 6 Nov 2026; nothing posted, nobody messaged,
nothing spent. Facts carry their source; where a group or number could not be confirmed it says so. Kamal does every
step that touches a student, a group or a college from his own accounts._

## The short version
1. **Students are not on Facebook.** Among 18 to 25 year olds in India, Instagram and WhatsApp lead and Facebook is
   fourth (IIM-Rohtak survey of 38,896 young people: Instagram 31%, WhatsApp 28%, Snapchat 20%, Facebook 17%, as reported
   by GrabOn; method not verified). Comscore's 2025 India review has Facebook users down about 5% in a year
   (Exchange4media). Public search found **no college Facebook group** for any Pune college. The college channels that
   exist are WhatsApp groups run by students, Telegram channels, Instagram pages, and the colleges' own student bodies
   (E-Cells, fest committees, student councils). This write-up follows the students, not the platform.
2. **The students who need us are the ones the hostels don't take.** Pune's hostels are small next to the intakes:
   PICT has roughly 200 to 400 hostel seats for 3,000 to 4,000 B.Tech students (Collegedunia, CollegeDekho); COEP's
   hostels hold about 1,486 to "over 2,000" and fill within two weeks of admissions (CollegeSearch, Zollege); Symbiosis's
   Viman Nagar hostels have 1,417 places for women and 118 for men (scmc.edu.in), so most men at SCMS, SLS, SID, SSLA and
   SCMC live out, with PGs "in the close vicinity and at Tingre Nagar" (scie.ac.in). Those students, and every second-year
   who leaves a compulsory first-year hostel, are our seekers and our listers (a room in a student flat opens every June).
3. **Fastest free route: the E-Cells and the student fests.** Pune's E-Cells run inter-college meetups and a startup
   fest, they exist to put student-facing startups in front of students, and a founder's talk or a "community partner"
   slot costs nothing. Pune Startup Fest at COEP is on **30 and 31 January 2027** (COEP's 2026-27 academic calendar).
4. **Biggest free reach: student ambassadors paid in the product.** One student per campus who shares a referral link
   and a value post in the groups they are already in. The app already pays referrals: every 2 friends who sign up
   with your link earn 1 free contact, up to 5 (README). No cash, no merchandise, no fees.
5. **The hold shapes everything.** Until Kamal lifts it the offer is "early access when we launch", so the aim this winter
   is a list of ambassadors, a few admin yeses and a fest slot, ready for the June to September rush, not sign-ups now.

## How each route is entered, what we offer, rules and risks

### 1. E-Cells
- **Entry.** Kamal emails or DMs the E-Cell (COEP: via the student-clubs page; MIT-WPU: the E-Cell page and its LinkedIn;
  SIBM Pune E-Cell: LinkedIn; Cummins Yukta: the college E-Cell page) offering a 30-minute founder session: "how we built
  a flatmate app in Pune, what we got wrong, and how to find a room without a broker". Ask to be a startup at their next
  inter-college meetup or summit.
- **We offer.** A real founder talk, early access for their members when we launch, and the deposit checklist as a
  handout they can post. Nothing else.
- **Rules.** Campus events go through the cell's faculty coordinator; the cell handles permission. Kamal speaks as a
  founder, not as an advertiser; no sign-up drive on campus, no student lists collected (see data rules below).
- **Risk.** Low. The worst case is no reply. Wait for the invitation; never turn up uninvited.

### 2. Student ambassadors
- **Entry.** Recruit from the E-Cell talks and the fest: one student per campus, second year or above (the first-years are
  in hostels and some are under 18). Written one-page terms, signed by nobody, that say exactly what they do: share the
  checklist and the referral link in groups they are already in, tell us which groups allow what, flag "looking for a
  room" posts. They never spam, never add people to groups, never collect numbers.
- **We offer.** What the app already gives: the referral reward (2 friends = 1 free contact, up to 5), early access, a
  named "campus ambassador" line for their CV and a LinkedIn recommendation from Kamal. Internshala's ISP and Unstop's
  programme recruit thousands on exactly this (certificate, perks, no fee; Unstop states it charges applicants nothing).
- **Rules.** Each campus group has its own admin and rules; the ambassador reads them first and reports them to the
  tracker. One post per group per week, varied wording, same as the Facebook rules in `pune-groups.md`.
- **Risk.** Medium. An over-eager ambassador gets removed and the name gets a reputation. Mitigation: the terms, a
  weekly five-minute check-in, and a stop rule (one warning in a group = stop in that group).

### 3. Fests (community partner)
- **Entry.** Fest committees form in November and December for January to March fests. Kamal emails the sponsorship or
  partnerships head (fest sites list them) asking for the free community-partner tier: our logo and a post from them, our
  post about them, early access for attendees. Pune Startup Fest'27 at COEP, 30-31 Jan 2027, is first; PSF'26 had a
  Startup Board Room and pitching formats (Campus Times, Free Press Journal), so a startup presence there is normal.
- **We offer.** Cross-promotion to our audience (Mark's Instagram), the founder on a panel if they want one, early access.
- **Rules.** Their sponsorship brochure is the rule book; the free tier is theirs to grant. No stall unless it is free.
- **Risk.** Low, apart from time. Many fests will say the free tier is for NGOs and communities; take the no.

### 4. Telegram and WhatsApp channels
- **Entry.** The official SPPU channel is one-way; skip. For the student-run channels, Kamal messages the listed admin
  once with the deposit checklist and asks if a useful post is welcome. The brief's rule holds: a group that forbids
  promotion gets an admin request, not a post, and a no is final.
- **We offer.** The checklist itself, written for students (deposit, notice period, police tenant verification).
- **Rules and risk.** These channels forward exam material; a flat post looks out of place. Low reach for us, low cost
  to ask once.

### 5. Instagram campus pages and Campus Times Pune
- **Entry.** Small campus pages (COEP Confessions, MIT Pune Memes) are run by students; a DM from Kamal offering the
  checklist as a carousel they can post, with a credit, is a favour they can grant. Campus Times Pune: pitch a story,
  not an ad, to contact@campustimespune.com.
- **Rules and risk.** The large Pune meme pages sell promotion; they are out of scope while the rule is zero spend.
  Instagram content is Mark's; Salu drafts the student version and hands it over.

## When: the student calendar
- **Second-years and above return in July**: SPPU's odd semester starts in the first week of July (third-party calendars
  give 1 to 7 July 2026; the official date varies by college). Rooms in student flats change hands in June and July.
- **First-year engineering joins late**: MHT-CET CAP 2026 registration ran 2 to 12 July, round-one options 28 to 30 July,
  four rounds in all with 1,52,452 admitted statewide (Shiksha). First-years arrive August to September, mostly into
  hostels; they become our audience a year later.
- **MBA batches**: June start is usual; not confirmed from an official page for SIBM or SCMHRD.
- **Fests**: January to March (Transcend, Sympulse, Symbhav, Pune Startup Fest) and September to October (MindSpark,
  Codemahotsav). Committees form two months before.
- So: **November to January** build the list (E-Cell talks, PSF'27 partner slot, first ambassadors); **February to May**
  keep the ambassadors warm; **June to September** is the only window that moves rooms, and the hold should be lifted
  before it.

## Rules and risks that apply everywhere
- **Age and data.** The app's age brackets start at 18 (README). The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025
  (notified 14 Nov 2025, fully in force by 14 May 2027) treat anyone under 18 as a child: verifiable parental consent,
  and no tracking or targeted advertising to children (India Briefing, EY, law-firm summaries; read the gazette text
  before relying on detail). First-years can be 17. So: no sign-up drives on campus, no collecting student lists or
  numbers, ambassadors from second year up, and a question for Teche below.
- **Police tenant verification.** Pune Police's May 2026 order makes tenant and PG reporting mandatory (Pune Pulse); on
  9 Oct 2026 Vishrambaug police gave PG operators in Budhwar Peth 24 hours to submit student details, made online tenant
  verification mandatory and asked for 60 days of CCTV (Punekar News). This is a value-post topic for students and
  owners, and a reason owners will want fitting, verifiable tenants.
- **Scams aimed at students' parents.** Pune cyber police reported fake PG offers sent to parents of newly admitted
  students, with money taken for rooms that did not exist (Free Press Journal, c. 2023). Our "never pay before you see
  it, numbers swap only on a yes" line is exactly the warning; it goes in every student post.
- **College permission.** Anything on campus goes through the student body and its faculty coordinator. Never a stall,
  poster or session without their written yes.
- **Platform rules.** Same as the city groups: no automation, no second accounts, no DMs to members, one post per group
  per week, stop after one warning. WhatsApp bans numbers that are added to many groups and send the same text; the
  ambassador posts from their own account, in groups they already belong to.
- **Reputation.** A founder who turns up to help students once and is never seen again does damage. Kamal commits to
  one E-Cell session a month through the season or we do not start.
