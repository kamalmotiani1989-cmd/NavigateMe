A flat looks its best for the ten minutes you are in it. The lights are on, the windows are open, and the person showing it would like you to say yes. None of that is dishonest. It just means the checking is your job.

This list goes in the order you will meet things: the room first, the money last. Tick it off as you go; your ticks stay on this phone.

## Before you go {#before}

Ask three things on the phone or in chat, so you don't cross the city for nothing: the rent **and** everything on top of it, the deposit, and who you would be living with. If any of the three gets a vague answer, expect vague answers later too.

Go in daylight. Damp patches, the view, and how much sun the room gets don't show at 8 pm. If you like it, come back or walk the lane once after dark before you commit: a street has two personalities.

## The checklist {#visit}

:::checklist visit | The flat-visit checklist
# The room
- The bed, mattress and cupboard that are promised are actually in the room
- The door locks, and I would get my own key
- There's a window that opens, and I know what it looks onto
- No damp patches, peeling paint or smell of mould, especially behind the cupboard
- Enough plug points, where I would need them
- My phone has signal in the room with the door shut, and data works
- I stood still for a minute and listened: road, neighbours, lift, generator
# Water and power
- The taps run with decent pressure, in the kitchen and the bathroom
- I asked when water comes, and whether there's a tank or a tanker
- There's hot water, and I know how: geyser, solar or bucket
- The flush works and the bathroom drains
- I asked about power cuts, and whether there's backup
- Every light, fan and AC I was shown works
# The kitchen and the common space
- The fridge, stove and washing machine work, and I know who owns them
- I know the kitchen rules, if there are any
- There's somewhere to dry clothes
- The common space is one I would actually sit in
# The building and the street
- The entrance, stairs or lift feel safe to come home to late
- I asked the guard or a neighbour what the building is like
- I know the society's rules: visitors, timings, parking, pets
- I checked the commute at the time I would really travel
- Food, a chemist and transport are within a walk
# The people
- I met everyone who lives there, not only the one showing me round
- We talked about sleep, cleaning, guests and noise
- I asked why the last person left
- I met or spoke to the owner, or I know exactly who they are
# The money and the paper
- Rent, and my share of it
- Everything on top: maintenance, electricity, water, Wi-Fi, cook, cleaner
- The deposit: how much, who holds it, and what comes off it when I leave
- Notice period and any lock-in
- Whether there's a written agreement, and whether my name goes on it
- Any brokerage, and who pays it
:::

## Three questions that tell you the most {#three}

If you only have five minutes, ask these.

:::steps
1. **"Why did the last person leave?"** A job in another city is a fine answer. A long pause is also an answer.
2. **"What does it cost in a normal month, everything included?"** The rent is one number. Maintenance, electricity, Wi-Fi and the cleaner are four more, and they are the ones people forget to mention.
3. **"What comes off the deposit when I leave?"** Painting, cleaning, a month's rent: whatever it is, you want to hear it now, not on the day you hand back the keys.
:::

More on the people side in [what to ask a flatmate before you say yes](post:what-to-ask-a-flatmate).

## Photograph everything {#photos}

Before you move a single box in, walk through with your phone. Walls, floors, the bathroom, every appliance, the meter reading. Send the photos to the owner or to the flat's group the same day, so they carry a date. It takes five minutes, and it is the difference between "that crack was there" and proving it.

## Before the token {#token}

A token holds the room while the paperwork is done. It is also the moment most people stop checking. Don't.

- **You have seen the flat yourself.** Not a video call, not a friend of a friend. You, inside it.
- **You know who you are paying, and why them.** The owner, or someone you have good reason to believe acts for the owner. A token to a person you met on a chat an hour ago is a gift.
- **It goes by bank transfer or UPI to that person's own account**, and you get a message or a receipt that says what it is for, how much, for which flat, and whether it comes back if things fall through.
- **The big numbers are in writing**, even if only in a chat: rent, deposit, move-in date.

:::warn If you are being hurried
"Three other people want it, pay now and see it later" is the opening line of most rental scams. A real owner with three real takers still lets you see the flat. [How rental scams work, and the red flags](post:rental-scams-red-flags).
:::

## After you say yes {#after}

Read the agreement before you sign it; [the basics are here](post:rent-agreement-basics). Then agree the money between flatmates: [how to split the rent fairly](post:split-rent-fairly) has a calculator for it.

A flat that survives this list isn't perfect. It is a flat with no surprises, which is the most you can ask of one.
