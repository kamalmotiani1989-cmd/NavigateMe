# Tests

`demo/` holds the browser suites that run against the app in demo mode (no backend: the demo data in `index.html`).
They cover the welcome page and sign-in card (`welcome`), onboarding (`ob`), the post form and the photo gallery (`pass3`), listing strength, reply speed, compare and
share (`p5`), Escape and FlatMart (`p6`), Flatsplits (`p7`), the short login page (`p25`), the band at the top of the phone's home screens and the bottom bar (`p26`: since 17 Nov the band is the page's cream, flat, with one key word of its headline in the orange), the Parties section and the bands on every screen (`p27`), speed, the one list of every home and the Escape's vibe score (`p28`), and the look of 16 Nov 2026 with the city step of sign-up, and the look of 17 Nov 2026 over it (`p31`: the palette, which is the logo's own orange, near-black, white cards and, since 17 Nov, a cream page; the serif headings, the orange buttons with white words, no light red, aubergine, older cream or peach tint left on any screen, every word readable, no panel melted into what it sits on; 17 Nov's key word in each main headline with the hand-drawn line on the first page and the story, the bigger headlines on small phones, the rent in the serif with its small rupee sign, the ink sheet on a home's page, the brown header and shared pictures, the Android bars; what the page-by-page audit found (progress, switches and focus in orange, one style of small capitals, the time box, the Passes pop-up on a computer), the share pictures, the privacy and Coming soon pages; and the logo, which is not part of the look: its files must match Kamal's originals of 9 Nov byte for byte, and every icon must be the orange tile; and, since 19 Nov, Tribe's two white cards: the people around you and the four steps), and the vibe score only where the lister lives there (`p32`: no score and "Vibe score after the household answers" on a room nobody lives in, nothing on a whole flat, the prompts in My flat and the Escape, and the posting form's questions following the count of people), and the posting form's "Do you live in this flat?" (`p33`: it must be answered; on Yes everything is as before; on No the lister's own answers and gender are not used, nobody listed is "You", and the home says "Owner-listed"; a listing with no answer is read as before and its editor asks), and Parties as a feed with a map (`p34`: the card with its drawn cover, "Tonight · 8 pm", the heart and how full it is; the search, the lenses and the order; "How was it?" for a party you went to; the party's page with its three steps and the "Ask to join" bar; the short message after asking, which says the host will let you know or, when the yes came back at once as a sample host's does, that you were accepted; "Host a party" on one sheet with the card people will see drawn as you fill it in; and the map, where a party is a circle about a kilometre across from a spot rounded to two decimals, never its pin, address or number). `p34` uses the bundled OpenStreetMap map and lets no request reach a map or search server, so the marks, circles and cards are checked and the map's pictures are not. The website's blog has a suite of its own (`p35`: that `blog/` is exactly what the articles in `marketing/blog/posts` say, by running `marketing/blog/build.js --check`; that it is website only; the first page's look, topics and search; every article's title, description, links, sources, sizes and contrast; the rent split calculator's sums; lists whose ticks stay; copying and WhatsApp; print; and less motion). `run.sh` serves `blog/` beside the app's page for it. Run them all with `tests/demo/run.sh`, or one with
`tests/demo/run.sh p7`; screenshots land in `/tmp/fm-demo-tests/shots`. Every check should pass; a `FAIL` line names
the check, and a suite that stops before its end (an error, or the time limit) counts as a failure too, with its
last words shown.

`p28` is the one suite that runs the page with the live backend switched on (`live.html`, a copy the runner makes):
it swaps the server client for a stand-in (`demo/stub/supabase-stub.js`) that answers every call after 300 ms from
made-up data and writes each call down, and it lets no request reach the real server. That is how the start-up
order and the number of server calls are checked. The suites need Node with Playwright's Chromium installed (`npx playwright install chromium` once) and
python3 for the static server on port 8765.

`demo/og-image.js` is not a suite: it draws `og-image.png`, the picture shown when someone shares the site's link
(how to run it is at the top of the file).

`db/` holds the database checks: `tests/db/run.sh` applies `supabase/schema.sql` twice to a fresh local Postgres (it
must be safe to run again) and then runs the `parties-*.sql` files, which call the functions as different people and
print `PASS` / `FAIL` lines (nearby parties, women-only homes, messages, block and report, avatars, and
`parties-15-listing-vibe.sql` for the living-style answers a listing carries where its lister lives there, and none where nobody does; both also cover the answer to "Do you live in this flat?"; and `parties-18-map-zone.sql` for what the Parties map gets, a spot rounded to two decimals and never the pin or the address before the host accepts, and for a party you went to staying in your list for three days after it ends; and `parties-19-samples.sql` for the 15 sample parties `fm_sample_parties` puts around a point: one of each kind, marked as samples, accepted straight away when you ask to join, notifying nobody, replaced when it is run again, and not callable from the app). How to point it at a local
Postgres is at the top of the script.

The browser-to-database suites (a bridge from the browser to a local Postgres) are not in the repository; the
schema's own checks are described in the README under "Security".
